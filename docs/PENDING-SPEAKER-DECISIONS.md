# Words of Christ — pending speaker decisions

These 21 decisions remain unresolved in the recovered working catalog. No inclusion decision is inferred by this consolidation.

1. **Psalms 75:10** — First-person speaker could be the psalmist or God; independent data marks God only as a potential speaker.
   INCLUDE if identified as God/premortal Christ; otherwise EXCLUDE

2. **Psalms 87:4** — The first-person “I will make mention” has disputed speaker identity; KJV does not explicitly identify whether God or the psalmist speaks.
   INCLUDE if identified as God/premortal Christ; otherwise EXCLUDE

3. **Proverbs 1:22–33; 8:4–36; 9:4–6, 9:11** — These are not explicitly identified by Proverbs as the LORD/Christ. Inclusion depends on whether the project identifies personified Wisdom as the premortal Christ.
   If Wisdom is identified as premortal Christ for this catalog, include the Wisdom speech ranges; otherwise leave them excluded.

4. **Isaiah 48:16** — The first-person speaker distinguishes himself from 'the Lord GOD' and says the Lord GOD and his Spirit sent him. Inclusion depends on identifying this speaker as the premortal Messiah/Christ.
   INCLUDE if this speaker is identified as the premortal Christ; otherwise EXCLUDE.

5. **Isaiah 50:4–9** — The speaker is the LORD's servant, not explicitly identified as the LORD in Isaiah. Inclusion depends on identifying the servant here as the premortal Messiah/Christ.
   INCLUDE the range if the servant is identified as premortal Christ; otherwise EXCLUDE.

6. **Isaiah 53:11 (partial speaker attribution)** — The phrase 'my righteous servant' can be read as the LORD speaking about the servant, but the KJV verse does not explicitly mark a speaker transition. This also raises a Father/LORD/servant identity distinction.
   INCLUDE only if the divine speaker is identified as the project’s premortal-Christ speaker; otherwise leave excluded.

7. **Isaiah 61:1–3** — Isaiah itself presents an anointed speaker rather than explicitly labeling the speaker as the LORD. Inclusion as Christ's words depends on Messianic identification.
   INCLUDE if treated as the premortal Messiah/Christ speaking; otherwise EXCLUDE.

8. **Isaiah 63:1–6** — The figure is divine/theophanic, but identifying the speaking warrior specifically as the premortal Christ rather than another divine referent is interpretive.
   INCLUDE if identified as premortal Christ; otherwise EXCLUDE.

9. **Jeremiah 13:15–23** — The KJV does not explicitly mark the speaker as the LORD throughout this unit; independent speaker data treats much of it as potentially either Jeremiah or God.
   INCLUDE if this unit is treated as an explicit LORD oracle spoken through Jeremiah; otherwise leave excluded.

10. **Jeremiah 17:11** — This proverb follows direct first-person LORD speech in 17:5–10, but the verse itself does not identify whether the LORD or Jeremiah is speaking.
   INCLUDE if treated as continuation of the LORD speech; otherwise EXCLUDE.

11. **Jeremiah 22:10** — This exhortation sits between LORD-oracle units. The immediate speaker can be read as Jeremiah rather than the LORD.
   INCLUDE if treated as part of the surrounding LORD oracle; otherwise EXCLUDE.

12. **Jeremiah 22:28–29** — These verses form a rhetorical/exhortative bridge immediately before 'Thus saith the LORD' in 22:30; speaker identity is not explicit.
   INCLUDE if treated as the LORD speaking through Jeremiah; otherwise EXCLUDE.

13. **Jeremiah 23:18–20** — The section lies between direct LORD speech units, but uses third-person reference to the LORD and can naturally be Jeremiah's commentary.
   INCLUDE if treated as continued divine oracle/relay; otherwise EXCLUDE.

14. **Jeremiah 29:24** — The command 'Thus shalt thou also speak...' likely introduces another LORD message, but the verse itself does not explicitly name the speaker.
   INCLUDE if treated as the LORD's instruction to Jeremiah; otherwise EXCLUDE.

15. **Jeremiah 30:23–24** — These verses are a third-person description of the LORD's whirlwind and anger after a long direct LORD speech; speaker transition is not explicit.
   INCLUDE if treated as continuation of the LORD oracle; otherwise EXCLUDE.

16. **Ezekiel 3:24–27** — The text transitions between “the spirit” and the Lord GOD. The message is plainly divine, but the immediate speaker/source identity is being kept separate rather than silently collapsed.
   INCLUDE as premortal-Christ speech if the project identifies this Spirit/LORD speaking unit with premortal Jesus; otherwise keep outside the strict Christ-speech layer.

17. **Ezekiel 8:5–18; 9:1** — The visionary speaker is variously attributable to the divine figure/God/Spirit; identifying the immediate speaker specifically as premortal Christ is interpretive.
   INCLUDE if the visionary speaker is identified as premortal Jesus; otherwise keep excluded from the strict set.

18. **Ezekiel 10:13** — The cry “O wheel” is spoken in Ezekiel’s hearing, but the immediate speaker is not identified in the KJV verse.
   INCLUDE only if the voice is identified as the divine/premortal-Christ speaker; otherwise EXCLUDE.

19. **Ezekiel 11:2–4** — The speaker immediately before the explicit Spirit-of-the-LORD attribution is not named with equal precision.
   INCLUDE if the pre-11:5 visionary speaker is identified as the same divine/premortal-Christ speaker; otherwise leave excluded.

20. **Ezekiel 40–42; 46:20,24; 47:6,8–12 (measuring-man speech)** — These sayings come from the measuring/bronze man of the temple vision. The text does not explicitly identify that figure as the LORD, so classifying his words as premortal Jesus requires a theophanic identification.
   INCLUDE these sayings only if the measuring/bronze man is identified as premortal Jesus; otherwise keep them outside the strict Christ-speech set.

21. **Daniel 8:16** — The commanding speaker is only identified as “a man’s voice between the banks of Ulai.” The voice orders Gabriel to make Daniel understand the vision, but the KJV does not identify that voice explicitly as God, the LORD, or Christ.
   INCLUDE as direct premortal-Christ speech only if this voice is identified with the divine/premortal-Christ speaker; otherwise keep the verse outside the strict set. Gabriel’s subsequent authorized interpretation remains included independently.
